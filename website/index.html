The chore

Eight steps, ten times a day.

Every time you sit down with a project it is the same ritual. Times dozens of projects, every day, forever.

loadcli does the eight. You do one.

  • open the Terminal
  • cd into the right folder
  • start the CLI
  • pick the model and the effort
  • open the browser
  • dig up the deploy URL
  • spin up a desktop so you don't clutter the others
  • drag and resize the windows
  • double-click a card

One click

What happens when you double-click.

Every window is verified. If one didn't land where it should, loadcli fixes it before handing control back to you.

01

You double-click

On the project's card. Or from the menu bar, without even opening the main window.

02

The desktop is born

A brand-new Space, on the monitor you chose. Your other desktops stay exactly as they were.

03

The terminal opens ready

Already in the project folder, running your CLI, on the model and effort that project asks for.

04

Everything takes its place

Browser at the deploy, a Finder folder, or just the terminal full screen. Focus ends where you are about to type.

What makes it different

The card lives inside the project folder.

There is no project database. Each folder carries a LOADCLI.md, and that file is the card. loadcli walks the folders you registered, finds those documents, and builds the grid from what they say.

 acme/LOADCLI.md
---
loadcli: 1
nome: Acme Site
grupo: Clients
icone: cart.fill
cor: "#3B82F6"
repositorio: github.com/acme/site
url: app.acme.com
cli: claude
modelo: opus
esforco: xhigh
---

The hard part

It creates desktops without you disabling SIP.

Creating a Space through a private API does not work in a modern macOS app. That is why tiling tools ask you to turn System Integrity Protection off.

loadcli does what a person would do: it drives Mission Control's + button through the Accessibility API, targets the monitor by its display id, and clicks the new desktop's thumbnail. Always verifying the result before moving on.

SIP stays intact. No daemon. No kernel hack.
